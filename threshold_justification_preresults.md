The rank decision uses two distinct thresholds because numerical nonzero sensitivity and useful information are not equivalent. A singular value is retained as numerically nonzero when its ratio to the leading singular value is at least 10⁻⁸, a declared computational cutoff used only to report numerical rank; practical distinguishability requires σ ≥ 1 after scaling the three qualitative mineral-probability outputs by their unit range and δ¹³C by the published 2.1‰ between-sample spread. Thus one screening-scale perturbation must produce at least one observation-scale change, while the 2.1‰ value is explicitly not treated as analytical precision. For the qualitative barite-pattern comparison, a case is called bone-localised only when the prior-averaged detection probability is at least 0.80 in the bone-proxy mask and at most 0.20 in the matrix-proxy mask. These cutoffs demand clear separation in the absence of a measured detection limit and registered sample masks; they are transparent screening conventions rather than significance levels or calibrated thresholds.
