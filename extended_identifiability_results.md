# Second-stage model-form and prior-envelope screen

The threshold justification was recorded separately before drafting this results narrative in `threshold_justification_preresults.md`. All calculations remain synthetic sensitivity tests. No rate, source term, endmember, or model form was fitted to Jian et al.'s observations.

## Barite pattern model-form test

At the benchmark Ba background, none of the three proposed configurations reproduces bone-restricted barite under the declared mask criterion. The acidity switch alone gives prior-averaged barite detection probabilities of 0.787 in the bone-proxy mask and 0.950 in the matrix-proxy mask. Adding the local Ba source at the benchmark background raises the bone-proxy probability to 0.945 but leaves the matrix-proxy probability at 0.809. Combining the source with acidity gives effectively the same barite probabilities. The acidity switch changes neither barite probability at meaningful precision and reduces matrix calcite inventory by only about 1.6% in this run; calcite inventory in the synthetic bone zone remains zero.

A separate, deliberately Ba-depleted stress case changes the background and inlet Ba scale to 10−3 of the benchmark, outside the continuous screening envelope. In that endmember the local Ba source alone, and the combined source-plus-acidity case, meet the categorical criterion, with probabilities 0.966 for the bone proxy and 0.199 for the matrix proxy under both permeability laws. Acidity contributes no detectable barite-pattern difference from local Ba supply alone. Across the source-rate sweep from 10−5 to 4×10−4 synthetic concentration units s−1, the same criterion is met in the low-background case; at 6×10−4 it is not. The corresponding total model barite inventory spans 4.15×10−8 to 1.80×10−6 mol m−2. Since the operator's threshold is relative to total model mineral inventory rather than an absolute measured detection limit, this is evidence only for a relative spatial pattern under an assumed Ba-depleted matrix. It does not establish that the predicted amount would be detectable or that such Ba depletion existed in the fossil.

## LHS rank distribution and δ13C closure

The 32-point paired LHS spans the 15 coordinates by one frozen screening unit in each direction. At the 20-cell mesh, the strict numerical rank is 3 in all 32 samples under Kozeny–Carman and all 32 under the power law. The practical rank is 1 in every sample under both laws. Singular-value ranges are 2.990–3.627 for mode 1, 0.0553–0.0782 for mode 2, 5.30×10−6–1.09×10−4 for mode 3, and zero for mode 4. The second and third directions remain below the practical cutoff. The resulting rank distribution is identical between permeability laws for this design. These are sample frequencies in a deterministic coverage design, not probabilities that the geological system has a given rank.

Propagating the declared carbon mixing and fractionation ranges yields δ13C predictions from −24.44 to −10.35‰, with median −17.47‰. Nine of 32 design points fall within the published concretion mean ±2.1‰ spread. The carbon-only closure has local rank one, and its normalized composite isotope singular value ranges from 2.990 to 3.627, above the practical cutoff in every LHS point. The joint screen therefore retains one practically distinguishable composite direction across this envelope, but does not identify the organic-derived fraction independently of the SRB offset, calcite-bicarbonate fractionation, and source endmembers. The 2.1‰ scale is between-sample spread, not measurement precision.

## Interpretation

The prior-envelope screen supports a stable count of one practically distinguishable direction for the specified synthetic model and screening ranges. The identity of that direction remains a mixture contrast, while the mechanical parameters remain structurally unobservable under one-way coupling. The model-form test does not robustly resolve the bone-only barite pattern: localization requires a Ba-depleted matrix condition outside the declared continuous Ba envelope, and the absolute barite inventory remains unconstrained. The next useful measurements are therefore spatially registered Ba and sulfate inventories or porewater constraints, plus absolute phase-abundance/detection-limit information, rather than an additional fit to the current pattern.
